# Interview with Maya (brand designer)

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time. Can you walk me through what happens after you finish a project?

**Maya:** Sure. I send the final files, then I send the invoice the same day. I have a template in my invoicing app, so that part takes maybe five minutes.

**Interviewer:** And then?

**Maya:** And then I wait. Honestly the waiting is the worst part. My terms are 30 days, but in practice it's more like 50 or 60 for the bigger clients.

**Interviewer:** What do you do when an invoice is late?

**Maya:** I hate chasing people for money. It feels like I'm begging for something I already earned. I usually write a polite email after a week, then another one, and by the third email I'm rewriting it five times so I don't sound angry.

**Interviewer:** How often does that happen?

**Maya:** Every month there's at least one client who's late. Last spring I had an agency that paid me 74 days after the invoice, and I had to dip into my savings to pay rent.

**Interviewer:** How did that affect you?

**Maya:** It's stressful because my income is already lumpy. I never know if next month is a good month or a scary month.

**Interviewer:** Have you tried any tools for reminders?

**Maya:** My invoicing app has automatic reminders, but I turned them off. They sounded robotic and I was worried a client would be offended. With small clients the relationship is everything.

**Interviewer:** If something handled the chasing for you, would you pay for it?

**Maya:** If it actually got me paid faster and didn't damage the relationship, yes, I'd pay ten or fifteen euros a month without thinking. But it has to sound like me.
